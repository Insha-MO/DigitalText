The door was too big.

I remember thinking that. Too big. Like everything else in this place. The walls. The shadows

I wanted to run. I wanted to wake up in the car with my parents, the wind in my hair, the radio playing something stupid.

But my parents were pigs now.

And I was standing in front of a door that was taller than our house.

I reached for the handle.

A face opened its eyes in the wood.

"You're the most pathetic little girl I've ever seen."

I didn't answer. I couldn't. My throat had closed up somewhere between the hall and the handle.

Then the door opened. And another. And another. A hallway inside a hallway inside a hallway. Like a dream that keeps folding in on itself.

A hand appeared. Old. Ringed. Nails like red claws.

"Come in."

I couldn't move.

So something moved me.

It grabbed me from the inside. Not my arm, not my shirt, something behind my ribs. The doors shut. Locked. I flew past gold rooms and painted vases and things I couldn't see.

Then I was falling.

I hit the floor hard. Rolled. Landed on my face.

I remember the carpet. Rough. Old. It smelled like dust and something sweet that had gone bad.

And then I saw her.

Massive. Wrinkled. A nose like a beak. Yellow hair piled high. Eyes that didn't blink.

Yubaba.

She didn't look up from her writing.

"Quiet down. You're making a racket."

I found my voice. Somehow.

"Could you give me a job?"

She waved her hand.

And my mouth zipped shut.

Like a zipper. Like I was a jacket. Like I was a doll she could open and close whenever she wanted.

I tried to open it. I couldn't.

She laughed.

"You're just a stinking, useless weakling."

I wanted to say: I know. I know I'm useless. I know I'm weak. But I have to try.

I couldn't say anything.

She lit a cigarette with her finger. Blew smoke from her nostrils.

"You could be a piglet. Or a lump of coal."

I was shaking. I didn't like that she could see me shaking.

But I couldn't hide it.

She laughed again.

"Who helped you, dear? Why don't you tell me?"

She waved her hand. My mouth opened.

And I screamed.

"Please! Can't you give me a job?"

"Don't start that again!"

"Please, I just want to work."

I don't know why I kept going. Every word felt like swallowing glass. Inside, I was screaming stop, just stop, run, find Haku, find a way out.

But my mouth kept moving.

I had to keep trying

She flew into the air. Papers flew off her desk.

Her nose was inches from my face.

"You're a lazy, spoiled crybaby. There's no job for you. Get out."

Her hand wrapped around my chest. Her nails dug into my throat.

"Or maybe I'll work you until you breathe your very last breath."

I remember thinking this is it. This is how it ends. Not in the car. Not at home. Here. In a place I can't believe is real.

Something rumbled.

A baby was crying. A giant foot kicked through a door.

"Oh, dear. You woke up the baby."

She ran to the door.

"Mommy's here. Be a good boy."

She looked back at me.

"Why are you still here? I told you to leave."

"I want you to give me a job, please!"

"Quiet down, you're scaring the baby!"

The foot kicked through the door again. Right into her face.

"Hi, sweetie. Don't be angry."

I didn't move.

"I'm not leaving till you give me a job!"

"Okay, okay, just be quiet."

A paper flew out of a fallen stack. A pen followed.

They floated to me.

"That's your contract. Sign your name away, and I'll put you to work."

"If I hear one little complaint, you'll join your parents in the pigpen."

契約書

Aburaya Bathhouse

The undersigned hereby agrees to serve the Aburaya Bathhouse.

Upon signing, the undersigned surrenders all claims to their former name. A new name shall be assigned by the proprietor, and by this new name the undersigned shall be known within these walls.

氏名 / Name: _____________________

I pointed to the paper.

"Do I sign here?"

"Just sign it. Do you want the job or not?"

No desk. No table. Just the tile floor by the fireplace.

I knelt down.

I didn't want to sign.

I didn't even fully understand what I'm signing.

I wanted to throw the pen and run.

But my parents were waiting to be dinner.

So I wrote my name.

C-h-i-h-i-r-o.

Seven letters. Mine. The name myy parents calls when they're looking for me in a crowd.

"I can't believe I took that oath. To give a job to anyone who asks. Ridiculous."

"Are you done yet?"

The paper flew out of my hands before I finished.

It landed in hers.

"So your name's Chihiro?"

"Yes, ma'am."

"What a pretty name."

She waved her hand above the ink.

Three characters floated up from the paper.

They hovered. Then they flew into her hand.

The paper was left with one.

"And it belongs to me now. From now on, your name is Sen. You got that?"

I stared at her.

Sen?.

That's not my name. That's not me. I'm Chihiro. I'm Chihiro. I'm—

"Answer me, Sen!"

"Yes, ma'am."

...

The door opened behind me.

"You called for me?"

Haku was standing at the entrance.

"This girl signed a contract. Set her up with a job."

Haku turned to me. His face was blank.

"Right. What's your name?"

"What, Chih — oh. It's Sen."

"Okay, Sen. Follow me."

I don't know who Chihiro is anymore.

I'm Sen.
